FAQ

Questions, answered

Learn more about entrepreneurship through acquisition, business succession, and our Brooklyn event.

What is the Silver Tsunami, and why does it matter to local businesses?

The Silver Tsunami refers to the growing number of Baby Boomer business owners approaching retirement. Many have spent decades building successful businesses but have no clear succession plan. When these owners retire without someone to take over, businesses may close, putting jobs, local services, and community relationships at risk.

AcquireLocal sees an opportunity to connect these legacy business owners with aspiring entrepreneurs who have the experience and ambition to carry their businesses forward.

What is entrepreneurship through acquisition (ETA)?

Entrepreneurship through acquisition (ETA) is a path to business ownership that involves purchasing an existing business rather than starting one from scratch.

Instead of developing a new business concept, aspiring entrepreneurs acquire an established operation that may already have customers, employees, revenue, and a track record.

For experienced professionals, ETA can be an opportunity to apply years of leadership, management, and industry experience to building something they own.

What size businesses does AcquireLocal focus on?

AcquireLocal focuses on established Main Street businesses valued at up to $10 million. These include independently owned businesses across industries such as retail, hospitality, professional services, and other sectors that contribute to local economies.

Our focus is on businesses with an existing customer base, operating history, and financial performance that aspiring entrepreneurs can evaluate as potential acquisition opportunities.

Some acquisitions may qualify for SBA-backed or other financing, depending on the business, the entrepreneur, and the transaction.

How can a legacy business owner begin exploring succession?

A good starting point is understanding your options and considering what you want for the future of your business. That may include transferring ownership to family members, employees, or an outside entrepreneur.

Business succession can involve financial planning, business valuation, identifying potential successors, and preparing for a transition.

AcquireLocal connects legacy business owners with education, resources, and specialists who understand business succession. You do not need to have made a decision about selling or transitioning your business to begin exploring your options.

Who should attend the November 12 Brooklyn event?

Our inaugural Brooklyn event brings together experienced professionals interested in business ownership, legacy business owners considering succession, lenders, attorneys, financial advisors, corporate partners, and community leaders.

Attendees will hear real experiences, learn about entrepreneurship through acquisition, explore financing and succession considerations, and connect with people who can help them take the next step.

Whether you are beginning to explore ownership, thinking about transitioning your business, or have expertise to contribute, we welcome you.

What are the current SBA financing requirements for acquiring a business?

The U.S. Small Business Administration offers loan programs that may help eligible entrepreneurs finance the acquisition of an existing business, including the SBA 7(a) loan program (opens in a new tab).

Eligibility and financing requirements depend on factors such as the applicant, business, purchase structure, creditworthiness, and applicable SBA rules.

For current requirements, visit the official SBA website or consult an SBA-approved lender. AcquireLocal can help aspiring entrepreneurs understand the financing landscape and connect with relevant resources and specialists. Financing approval is determined by the lender and applicable program requirements.
